The method

Eight steps, from site study to installation, each one checked before the next begins. Examples: Boëlhe in Hesbaye, Corroy-le-Château and Bois-Seigneur-Isaac.

Site study and flight plan

Each site is studied before the visit: terrain, vegetation, building heights, airspace restrictions. The flight is then calculated point by point, with the angle and distance of every shot.

Aerial survey

A single site visit of one to two hours, with the owner's written consent. The drone follows the calculated plan and covers the whole building, roofs and façades, from many angles.

Height referencing

Measurements are cross-checked against airborne laser surveys. Eaves and ridge heights, roof pitches and terrain relief are fixed to within a few decimetres. At Corroy, every slate roof plane was measured individually.

Photogrammetric reconstruction

The shots are reconstructed in three dimensions and georeferenced. The result is a top view at 3 centimetres per pixel and scaled façade surveys on which every opening is counted and dimensioned.

Modelling

The building is entirely redrawn from the measurements, volume by volume, down to the dormers, chimneys and bridges. The terrain is reproduced with its real relief.

Conformity check

The model is compared with the real shots, façade by façade. Every discrepancy is found and corrected before production.

Preparing for production

The model is broken down into parts that fit together to a tenth of a millimetre, so the joints stay invisible once assembled.

Production and assembly

The parts are made in layers of about a tenth of a millimetre, in white or in two tones, then mounted on a wooden base with an engraved name plate.
